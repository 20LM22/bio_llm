## 2. Decide what STL can represent well

STL is good at representing:

- whether it is increasing before or during that interval

- **T-cells becoming detectable** → represented as `tcells(t) > c(low)` or similar
- **within two weeks of symptom onset** → represented as a temporal interval `[0,14]` if we set symptom onset to time 0

This is a **population qualifier**, not a time-varying signal in the grammar.

### b. “SARS-CoV-2-specific T-cells”

### c. “appear”

- previously absent or low
- then becomes detectable/present

- **eventually** `tcells(t) > c(low)`, or

The second option better captures the idea of emergence rather than a one-time noisy blip.

### d. “in peripheral blood”
This is about the measurement compartment. STL here has no explicit compartment variable, so this is also treated as **measurement context**, not encoded.

### e. “symptom onset”
This is an external clinical event, not one of the listed species.  
The standard STL workaround is to **define time 0 = symptom onset**. Then “within two weeks of symptom onset” becomes the interval `[0,14]`.
